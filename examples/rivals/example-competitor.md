# Rival dossier — Tempora

> **Fictional competitor — this file demonstrates the dossier shape.** Tempora does not exist; every claim, quote, and price below is invented for illustration. In a real dossier, every claim cites a captured URL + date, backed by snapshots under `raw/<slug>/<YYYY-MM-DD>/`.

## Snapshot: 2026-08-16

**Positioning (their words).** Homepage H1: "Time tracking that runs itself." Subhead: "Tempora watches your calendar and apps, logs your hours, and turns them into invoices — no timers." The whole page is framed as automatic-vs-manual.

**Features / capabilities — vs ours.**

| Capability | Tempora | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic time capture (calendar + app watching) | Yes — the core pitch | No — manual timers |
| Invoicing | Basic (PDF export only) | Full: payment links, reminders |
| Client portal | No | Yes |
| Offline / local data | No — cloud only | Yes — local-first |
| Integrations | Google Calendar, Slack | Calendar file import only |

**Pricing + packaging.** Free (1 client) · Pro $9/mo (unlimited clients, invoicing) · Team $19/user/mo. 30-day trial on Pro. Single value axis: client count.

**Who they sell to.** Freelance designers and developers; every testimonial is a solo operator. The Team tier looks aspirational — no team-shaped features behind it.

**Strengths to respect.** Automatic capture is a genuinely better wedge than timers — nobody likes timers. Onboarding is strong: tracked hours appear before you've configured anything.

**Weaknesses to exploit.** Cloud-only app-watching is a privacy story that worries exactly their audience. Invoicing is a checkbox, not a workflow (no payment links, no reminders). Nothing between $9 solo and a team tier the features don't justify.

**Counter-or-adopt.**
- **Adopt:** calendar-based auto-suggestion of time entries — their best idea, buildable local-first.
- **Counter:** lead with "your hours never leave your machine" against their app-watching.
- **Ignore:** the Team tier — no evidence of pull.

## Snapshot: 2026-08-17

**Positioning (their words).** H1 changed: "Time intelligence for independents." New subhead leads with "an AI weekly summary of where your time went." The word "invoice" has left the fold.

**Features / capabilities — vs ours.** New: "AI Weekly Review" — a summary email of tracked time (Pro and up). Everything else unchanged from 2026-08-16.

**Pricing + packaging.** Pro raised $9 → $12/mo; trial shortened 30 → 14 days; Free tier unchanged. New "Founding" badge on Pro annual at $99/yr.

**Who they sell to / strengths / weaknesses.** Unchanged from baseline, except: the AI-summary pivot reads as chasing engagement (a weekly email) over billing depth — invoicing now looks even more like a checkbox.

**Counter-or-adopt.**
- **Watch:** if the "time intelligence" repositioning sticks, invoicing becomes ground they've conceded.
- **Counter:** payment-link invoicing is now a clean differentiator — say it louder on the pricing page.

### What changed since 2026-08-16
- **Repositioned:** "Time tracking that runs itself" → "Time intelligence for independents"; invoicing de-emphasized off the fold.
- **Price up:** Pro $9 → $12/mo; trial 30 → 14 days; new $99/yr founding annual.
- **Feature added:** AI Weekly Review (Pro+).
- **Read:** monetizing the AI narrative while quietly conceding the billing workflow — the gap we occupy widened.
